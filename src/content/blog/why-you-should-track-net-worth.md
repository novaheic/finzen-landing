People obsess over income. Salary negotiations, side hustles, passive income streams — the focus is always on earning more. But income is only half the equation. What matters is what you keep.

Two people can earn the same salary and end up in completely different financial positions. The difference is almost always spending and saving behavior, not income level. Net worth captures that full picture.

## What is net worth?

Net worth is simple: everything you own minus everything you owe.

**Assets** (cash, investments, property, retirement accounts) minus **liabilities** (credit card debt, student loans, mortgage) equals your net worth.

That single number tells you more about your financial health than your salary, your credit score, or your monthly budget combined.

## Why it matters

**It is the only honest number.**
Income can be misleading. Someone earning €120,000 with €80,000 in debt and no savings is in a worse position than someone earning €50,000 with €30,000 invested and no debt. Net worth does not lie.

**It shows progress over time.**
Monthly income fluctuates. Expenses spike. But net worth, tracked monthly, reveals the trend. Are you moving forward or backward? The line answers that question without ambiguity.

**It connects spending to consequences.**
When you see how a purchase affects your net worth — not just your checking account — spending decisions feel different. That €1,200 phone is not just a budget line item, it is a visible dip in your wealth trajectory.

**It motivates long-term thinking.**
Watching your net worth grow, even slowly, creates a positive feedback loop. Each month's number becomes a score you want to beat. That psychological pull toward building is more powerful than any budget rule.

## How to track it

1. **List every asset with its current value.** Bank accounts, brokerage accounts, retirement funds, crypto, property estimates.
2. **List every liability.** Credit cards, loans, mortgages — include them all.
3. **Subtract liabilities from assets.** That is your net worth today.
4. **Update monthly.** Same day each month. Consistency matters more than precision.

The first number might be negative. That is okay. What matters is the direction. A net worth that goes from -€5,000 to -€2,000 in six months represents real, measurable progress.

## Beyond the budget

Budgeting tells you where your money goes this month. Net worth tells you where your life is going this decade. Both matter, but if you are only tracking one, track net worth. It is the metric that connects daily decisions to long-term outcomes.
